About The Follow-Through

An independent, non-commercial tracker of EU decisions. Brussels makes the promises; we follow what happens next.

What we do

Every day, a program reads the EU's official journal and lists the main decisions taken in the last two years: laws passed by the European Parliament and the Council, Council regulations and directives, and foreign-policy decisions such as sanctions, missions and military aid. For each one we show its legal milestones (entry into force, deadlines), what came next (follow-up acts, reports, amendments) and, when it serves a measurable EU target, how far Europe is from that target according to official statistics.

The Follow-Through is not affiliated with the European Union or any of its institutions, and is not funded by any of them.

How we score

Goal progress: when a decision serves a numbered EU target (for example, cutting greenhouse gas emissions by 55% by 2030), the score shows how far Europe has come between a starting year and the goal, using Eurostat data. “On track” compares the pace of the last ten years with the pace needed to reach the goal in time. See the Scorecard.

Rollout: otherwise, the score is the share of the decision's legal milestones that have already passed. It says whether a decision is in force, not whether it works.

Limits and corrections

Everything here is produced automatically, including the headlines, which are written by rules from the official titles. Mistakes are possible. This site is for general information only and is not legal advice: the official texts on EUR-Lex are the only authentic versions, and every decision page links to them.

Spotted an error, or want a photo removed? Tell us by opening an issue on our public GitHub page: github.com/thefollowthrough/thefollowthrough.github.io/issues (a free GitHub account is needed). We correct errors as quickly as we can.

Privacy

  • No cookies, no advertising, no tracking or audience measurement, no account to create.
  • Fonts are served from this site: your browser does not contact Google to display the page.
  • The site is hosted by GitHub Pages. Like any web host, GitHub records technical data such as IP addresses in its server logs (see the GitHub privacy statement).
  • Photographs are loaded from Wikimedia's servers, which therefore receive your IP address (see the Wikimedia privacy policy).
  • The “Share” button uses your device's own sharing menu or copies the link: nothing is sent to us.
